Stripe Checkout · $20 Saturday Registration Fee

Online payment for the $20 Saturday fee.

Right now the form just collects an acknowledgment that the $20 is paid in cash or check at check-in. Stripe Checkout adds a card-payment option so attendees can pre-pay during registration. This page tracks the setup work on both sides — Zion's account-owner tasks (treasurer / Pastor Scott) and the developer tasks I'll handle once we're given the green light.

Owner: Zion Lutheran (treasurer) + Gary Ricke (developer)  ·  Target: Post-launch, June 2026
Zion's steps

Zion Lutheran — starting from scratch

No coding, no technical skill. Plan about 15–20 minutes the first time, a 1–2 day wait while Stripe approves the account, then 5 minutes to flip the switch when I tell you we're ready. Each step below has one thing to do.

Gather these before you start
  • Zion's EIN (the federal tax ID — the treasurer has this)
  • Zion's official mailing address
  • Zion's bank routing & account number (treasurer has these too)
  • Name, birthdate, and SSN of one authorized officer (Pastor Scott or a board member). Stripe is legally required to ask — it stays private.
  1. 1
    Sign up for Stripe.

    Go to stripe.com and click the "Start now" button in the top-right corner. Use a church email (not a personal Gmail) so anyone at Zion can get back in later. Pick a password and write it down somewhere safe.

    About 5 minutes. Signing up is free. Stripe only takes a small fee when someone actually pays.
  2. 2
    Tell Stripe who Zion is.

    Stripe will ask the questions from your prep list above — Zion's name, address, EIN, and the authorized officer's info. Fill them in carefully and click Submit.

    Stripe needs this so they know they're sending money to a real organization — it's the same kind of check a bank does. They'll review and email you when you're approved, usually within 1–2 business days.
  3. 3
    Tell Stripe where to send the money.

    In the same setup flow, Stripe asks for Zion's bank routing and account number. This is where registration deposits will land — the same account Zion uses for direct-deposit donations.

    Out of every $20 registration, Stripe keeps about 88¢ as their processing fee. Zion gets $19.12. There's no monthly cost — only when someone pays.
  4. 4
    Pick a deposit schedule and a statement name.

    Two quick questions in the setup screens:

      • How often do you want deposits? Choose Daily (the default). You'll see deposits the next business day after someone pays.

      • What name should show on attendees' credit-card bills? Type ZIONLUTHERAN CR20. (Short on purpose — Stripe has a character limit.)

  5. 5
    Add Gary so he can build the payment form.

    Once you're approved, click Settings → Team → Invite Members. Type Gary's email — gary.ricke@orbisdesign.com — and pick the role Developer. Click Send Invite.

    30 seconds. This lets Gary do the technical setup without you having to share your password.
  6. 6
    Let Gary know you're ready.

    Send a one-line text or email: "Stripe is set up — you can build it now." That's all he needs.

    Gary will then build the registration → payment form and test it using fake card numbers. No real money moves until step 7.
  7. 7
    Click "Activate live mode" when Gary says it's ready.

    When Gary finishes testing, he'll send a short note: "Ready to accept real payments." Log in to Stripe and click the "Activate live mode" button in the top-right corner of the screen. That's the moment real card payments start working.

    We save this click for you (not Gary) so there's a clear paper trail showing the church authorized going live.
  8. 8
    Decide who handles refunds.

    If someone asks for their $20 back, refunding it is one click inside Stripe. Decide ahead of time who's allowed to do that — usually Liz or the treasurer. Gary will show that person how in under a minute.

  9. 9
    Once a month, glance at Stripe vs. the bank statement.

    The treasurer logs into Stripe, sees the list of deposits Stripe sent to Zion, and confirms the totals line up with the bank statement.

    Five-minute job, once a month. Keep a copy with regular church financial records.
My steps

Gary's developer tasks

All of this can happen in Stripe test mode in parallel with Zion's account work — but nothing goes live until Zion finishes steps 1–4 above.

  1. 1
    Create the $20 product in Stripe.

    Stripe Dashboard → Products → "CR 20th Anniversary — Saturday Registration", $20.00 USD, one-time. Build it in test mode first.

  2. 2
    Choose Payment Link vs. Checkout Session.Decide

    Payment Link (Stripe hosts the page, zero code) is fastest and lets us launch payment for late registrants with one URL. Checkout Session (small Netlify Function) gives us the registrant's name/email pre-filled and a clean post-pay redirect. Recommend Payment Link to start; upgrade later if needed.

  3. 3
    Wire the form's Saturday block to the Stripe URL.

    Replace (or augment) the cash/check acknowledgment with a "Pay $20 now" button that opens the Stripe-hosted Checkout. Cash/check stays as the fallback for anyone who'd rather pay at the door.

  4. 4
    Configure success + cancel URLs.

    Success: thank-you.html?paid=1&session={CHECKOUT_SESSION_ID}. Cancel: back to #register.

  5. 5
    Webhook → Google Sheet update.

    Add a webhook endpoint (Netlify Function or extend the existing Google Apps Script) listening for checkout.session.completed. On fire, flip the registrant's row in Liz's sheet to Payment status: Paid and attach the Stripe receipt URL.

  6. 6
    Test-mode smoke test.

    Use Stripe's test card 4242 4242 4242 4242 (any future expiry, any CVC). Verify: form submits, Checkout opens, payment succeeds, thank-you page loads, webhook fires, Sheet row updates.

  7. 7
    Walkthrough with Liz.

    Five-minute screen-share so Liz sees what attendees see — and how she'll view paid vs. unpaid rows in her sheet.

  8. 8
    Flip to live keys.After Zion 1–4

    Replace STRIPE_SECRET_KEY_TEST with the live key in Netlify env vars. Update the Payment Link / Session URL on the site to point at the live product.

  9. 9
    Live smoke test ($20 real transaction, then refund).

    One real card payment to confirm everything works end-to-end. Refund it from the Stripe Dashboard after — zero net cost.

  10. 10
    Verify first payout in Zion's bank.

    Wait one payout cycle (default daily after first); confirm the deposit lands in Zion's operating account. Take a screenshot for the handoff doc.

  11. 11
    Handoff doc for Liz / treasurer.Nice-to-have

    One-page PDF covering: log in to Stripe, view today's payments, issue a refund, pull a monthly report, and who to contact (Gary) if anything looks wrong.

Why deferred until after launch?

The campaign is launch-critical between now and June 5–6. Stripe identity verification takes 1–2 business days at the church side, and there's no margin in the current schedule for that to slip. Cash/check at check-in is a proven fallback. Once registrations start flowing through the existing form, we'll know the actual demand and can prioritize online payment if it matters.

Estimated time once we're green-lit: ~2–4 hours of dev work on Gary's side (most of it is testing), plus 1–2 business days waiting on Stripe identity verification if Zion creates a fresh account.
